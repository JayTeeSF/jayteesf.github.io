The full ladder — what closed, what's new

July 30 · shipping v0.99.473a, the 474a fold mid-chain · companion to the July 29 ladder

Closed since the July 29 post

  • P1 is done — the type-guessing catalogue is closed. Three landings (471a store move-semantics, 472a boxed arithmetic, 473a has/has?), each proven on the shipping binary and certified on a second machine. a * 2 is 3.0, (a) * 2 stopped string-repeating, has() through a function stopped crashing. No catalogued site lets a spelling decide semantics anymore.
  • Fannkuch ran correctly in Hey for the first time — and got a full autopsy the same day. Honest score: 161× slower than C. Cause, proven not guessed: two heap allocations per array store, both malloc sites named by backtrace. Three plausible mechanisms were proposed and killed by measurement on the way; every retraction published at claim volume.
  • Every Hey web app now does less work per request. Six per-request middleware scans were per-service constants; they now resolve once at serve time (37 → 20 internal reads per request, measured on both machines, gate fully green for the first time since 469a). Folding into 474a as this posts.
  • The name-collision design is ruled, in full. Your names always win — data keys and function names both; overriding a builtin warns (suppressible only by an explicit marker in code); ergonomic defaults become an ordinary forkable Sys module, not a hidden prelude. A system-wide census (40,376 files) sized the migration honestly: ~191 live files would see the warning; a hard error would have bricked every shipped dist, so: warning.
  • The test harness now guards itself. Four instrument-defect classes it caught in its own gates this week — can't-parse, can't-execute, zero-byte silence, and registered failure signatures that could never fire — each got a structural guard. One of those silent gates had been hiding a real bug: non-finite floats printing INT64_MIN with exit 0 in one lane. It now announces itself, with its mechanism, in every run.
  • The web speed claim re-verified on the new binary: 1.099× Go per core (n=8, CI ±1%) — unchanged through all three landings.

Version-discipline audit, since the 2.6 slip could have infected other claims: the published benchmark articles cite Ruby 3.2/3.4 rows, and the 1BRC bars (~12 min Ruby) transfer from the challenge's published results, not from our own old-Ruby runs — so yesterday's fannkuch note was the only old-Ruby row, and it's corrected. New standing rule regardless: every Ruby row prints its version, and only current stable qualifies for a published table.
