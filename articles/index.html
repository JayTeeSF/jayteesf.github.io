  • Why Hey compiles slowly — 24 October 2026

    A picture-first decision page. A real Hey self-compile takes about 16 minutes and 14,700 G instructions; the 63.4 s quoted for weeks was one stage, about 6%. Every stage grows 2.5-3.4x when the program doubles. Where the time goes (text re-scanned and re-parsed, values copied), what Zig, Go, Carbon and TinyCC do instead, the four fix branches running now, two new streams for emit-llvm memory and narrowing, estimates against the 60% target, and the Maintainer's answers to three questions (Q1 measure first, Q2 yes, Q3 yes).

    2026-10-24
  • Who decides when a value is freed — 23 October 2026

    A picture-first decision brief. A map put stored inside a branch is copied on every update today: 80,000 updates take 88 s instead of 0.02 s, because ownership travels as text notes and the emitter decides most releases itself. The Perceus-style plan, not yet built: MIR names every value, writes dup and drop at last use, a separate verifier checks them, the emitter obeys. Nine slices, what depends on call identity, and six decisions.

    2026-10-23
  • Calling C from Hey directly — 23 October 2026

    A picture-first decision page. Calling C functions directly for 0.99.606a, why that stays inside the compiler’s rules, what the 16 native packages must do, the plan, and five decisions for the Maintainer.
