Problem A: every array write copies the array

The measurements are in and they agree. What is left is not a measurement question — it is a language question, and it is yours. Two decisions, plus a sequencing call.

Hey's IPC is nearly double C's — the processor is not stalling, it is faithfully executing far too many instructions. That removes prefetching, allocator swaps, NUMA and governor tuning from the table entirely. hey_value_clone deep-copies on every array write, so set x = put(x, i, v) rebuilds the whole array.

The fix already exists — hey_rc_cow_enabled, hey_runtime.c:1248 — and has never once run. Turning it on moves RSS by 0.008%. If clone had become a refcount bump, allocation would have collapsed. It did not. Every put(x,i,v) also reads x, the uniqueness test fails, and the deep copy is taken anyway.

Open risk that could change the priority

On Linux at one worker, Hey served 22,991 rps against Go's 107,735 — roughly 4.7× slower per core. That does not sit comfortably beside the 07-27 certification of “within 3% of Go, per core.” I have not verified the endpoints are comparable and am not claiming the certification was wrong. But if it turns out to have been taken at one core and expressed per-core, it was structurally blind to this — and Problem A becomes more urgent than that certificate implies, not less.

Decision 1 — the 4×, and how much correctness risk to carry

The blocker is not performance, it is soundness: a uniqueness predicate that must survive a read-borrow. Two TSAN races were already measured on the i5c flip on 07-29. If in-place fires when the array is not truly unique, we do not get a slow program — we get a wrong one.
