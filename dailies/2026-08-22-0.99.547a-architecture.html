Hey 547a: same three IRs, one authority converging.

The three-representation compiler did not change shape since 533a. What changed is who owns cross-unit meaning. The name resolver now hands the middle IR semantically-neutral extern facts; the Hey-owned emitter lowers them mechanically; and a resolved cross-unit comparison now travels the same semantic authority path as every other comparison. This is convergence, not a redesign.

Source checkpoint: 0.99.547a, commit 2e6313f8 Tree: clean, branch trunk Measured: 2026-08-22

TL;DR

  • Verdict: the core three-IR architecture — source → tokens → tree → HIR → MIR → LLVM — is unchanged. 547a is authority-convergence progress: the resolver-to-MIR extern bridge has landed, and one of 533a's two blocking gates has moved decisively.
  • Strongest result: the Hey-owned emitter now covers 111 of 132 corpus units, with 2 fallback and 19 fail-to-build — freshly re-measured from 547a trunk with sh tools/hey_llvm_gap_census.sh.

What changed since 533a

The 533a report ended on a clean sentence: the new front end exists, but two gates still block one authority. The emitter could not lower the whole structural program, and cross-unit meaning was still being reconstructed downstream instead of established once and carried forward.

Since then this work has done one thing well: it made cross-unit calls flow the way single-unit code already did. The name resolver knows every module signature. Until recently that knowledge died at the resolver boundary, and the backend guessed the rest. Now the resolver writes those signatures into the MIR as plain, semantically-neutral facts — an extern line, or a normalized Module.method receiver — and downstream stages trust them instead of re-deriving them.

That is the whole shape of the update. The architecture did not gain a fourth IR or lose one. It moved a decision earlier and stopped repeating it.

Why json_call is a lowering choice, not a semantic one

The important discipline here is the one that keeps the architecture honest. The extern fact in the MIR says what is being called and with what shape. It does not say how the backend must reach it. Today the Hey-owned emitter reaches it through a generic json_call dispatch. The MIR source itself records the plan in a comment: json_call dispatch today, real hey_fn_* symbols later.

That separation is why the flip is cheap. When the emitter later resolves these to direct hey_fn_* symbols, nothing in the resolver, the HIR, or the MIR needs to move. Only the lowering step changes. The language semantics were fixed the moment the resolver wrote the fact down.

Two rulings keep the mechanism from over-reaching:

Option A: modules are not runtime values

A module is a compile-time selector, never a first-class value you can pass or store at runtime. Receiver-sugar module calls are canonicalized at resolve time, so the backend never has to model a module as data.

Native facade wins precedence

Where a dedicated native-C facade lowering exists for a call, it takes precedence over the generic extern→json_call route. The mechanical path is the fallback for the frontier, not a replacement for hand-tuned facades.

The comparison slice: evidence the fact reaches the authority

The most recent landing (2e6313f8) is small and telling. A resolved boxed cross-unit call used to sit outside the normal comparison machinery. Now an expression of the form <resolved extern call> OP <literal> enters the same relational-comparison path as any other boxed comparison, through the shared helper hey_llvm_cmp_expr_value, which dispatches to hey_llvm_value_cmp_i64 — the runtime-type-aware semantic authority.

The observable payoff: two corpus units, bits_flags and bloom_filter, flipped from fail to covered because their cross-unit comparisons stopped needing a special case. That is the convergence thesis in miniature — a resolved fact routed to the one authority instead of a bespoke arm.
