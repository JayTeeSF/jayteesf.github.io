The premise, corrected. x.to_i on nil does not crash in Hey — it silently returns 0 (nil.to_f→0.0, nil.to_s→"nil"; the builtins have no nil branch, hey_runtime.c:14571/14592). So the real hazard is the quietest form of Hoare's billion-dollar mistake — a plausible wrong answer, no stack trace. A safe operator alone fixes nothing while the unsafe path still returns 0. So the root decision is D50 (what nil.to_i does), not the operator.

Two field-wide invariants (Ruby, Kotlin, Swift, C#, Rust): the safe chain short-circuits to nil at the first nil link, and the safe expression's type is nullable, never bare T. And Hey has no closures — which rules out lazy combinator methods (map/and_then/unwrap_or_else), but not a short-circuiting ?? operator (that lowers to plain control flow, like and/or).
