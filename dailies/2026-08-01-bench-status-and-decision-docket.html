The finding that reframes the rest

At c=32 both lanes are concurrency-bound, not core-bound: c/latency predicts the throughput in each (Hey 32/237µs = 135,021 against 133,023 observed; Go 32/192µs = 166,667 against 157,511). When the generator's own setting reproduces the number, the number is not a capability ceiling. Every “~132K” figure on record — mine, the certification's, and the published ones — is an artefact of that setting. Sweeping concurrency until each lane stops rising is what produced the 161,645 / 178,325 pair above.

The decisions

Five. The first two are the ones that change what the public sees, and they are the reason this page exists rather than a bus message.

Decision 1

The published “single core” numbers are single-worker numbers

Hey at one worker uses 4.21 cores. Go at one core uses 0.97 and delivers the same throughput. The measurement is sound; the label is wrong, and it is wrong on the public site.

Decision 2

The certification comparator itself

Hey/Go 1.0991 at c=32 is the standing published ratio. c=32 is concurrency-bound, so it compares two lanes at a setting neither had saturated. At true peaks the ratio is 0.9065 — the sign flips.

Decision 3

What I do next, while the box is busy and the release is pending

Everything timing-shaped needs a quiet box that has not existed since 29 July. These are the things that do not.

Decision 4
