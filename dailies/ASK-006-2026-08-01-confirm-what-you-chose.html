Negative division

-7 / 2

  now      -3
  you said -4      (same as Ruby)

Also changes -7 % 3 from -1 to 2.

Costs 15% speed on our benchmark.

Why it costs 15%

The chip's divide instruction rounds toward zero. Rounding toward negative infinity means adding a correction after every divide, and that correction can't be lifted out of a loop.

Two things soften it. Dividing by 2, 4, 8, 1024 and so on gets faster — 30-45% — because rounding toward zero is what needs the fixup there, not rounding down. Only odd divisors pay.

And the 15% is on a loop where the correction is never actually needed — the numbers are always positive — but the compiler can't prove that. Building the analysis that proves it is real work we haven't done.

Your call:

A. Take it. Match Ruby, accept 15%.

B. Wait. Keep -3 for now, do the speed work first, match Ruby later.

I'd take A. Linux will likely be cheaper than 15%, and we passed the speed bar with room.
