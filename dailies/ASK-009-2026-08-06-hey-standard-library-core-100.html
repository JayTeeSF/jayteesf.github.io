The standard library is 281 functions past a hard limit

You asked me to aim for a core of about 100 functions and push the rest into packages. Before commissioning that I counted, and two things changed the shape of the request: the library is four times larger than the brief assumed, and roughly half of it is not library at all — it is the compiler implementing itself. Five rulings, and nothing gets extracted until they land.

The correction, first

Correction to the opening estimate

The brief said "about 500–600" functions. Counted on the tree today, the public surface is 2,329 across 111 modules, with 679 more private ones, for 3,008 declarations in total. The estimate was out by roughly four times, and in the direction that makes the problem worse rather than better.

The limit is real and I found it: HEY_REPL_MAX_FUNCTIONS is 2,048, a fixed array in runtime/hey_repl.c. So the public surface already exceeds it by 281.

The part that should worry us more than the count

What the limit does on overflow is print one line to stderr — too many function definitions — and then silently discard the definition. The function is simply not registered, so nothing fails at that moment. It fails later, somewhere else, as an unknown function, with nothing connecting it back to the cause.

A capacity limit that drops work instead of stopping is its own defect, and shrinking the standard library does not fix it — it only moves us back under the ceiling so the trap is armed again rather than sprung. I have filed it separately. Ruling S5 is whether it gets fixed now or later.

The split that changes the target

The 2,329 is two very different populations sharing one namespace:

PopulationPublic functionsWhat it is
Compiler implementation1,129HeyC 231, HeyLlvm 152, HeyHir 122, HeyParser 118, HeyCompilerNodes 117…
User-facing API1,200Collections 55, Files 48, Text 43, Cli 43, Net 40…

Half the "standard library" is Hey compiling Hey. Those are not functions a programmer calls; they are the self-hosting machinery, and they are in the table only because they live in stdlib/. That matters for the target: a proposal that reaches 100 by counting the compiler as "moved to a package" has not answered your question, and a proposal that treats HeyLlvm like Text will break self-hosting.

So "100 most important functions" is a decision about the 1,200, not the 2,329 — and the compiler half needs a different answer, which is S2.

What I have commissioned already

Research is briefed and gated: five markdown documents, one each for Go, C++, Ruby, Python and Rust, written to recover two things rather than inventory features — the inclusion principle each language stated, and the exclusion mechanism it uses to keep the rest out and still usable. Python's PEP 594, which argued and executed the removal of stdlib modules, is the single most relevant document to this decision and is called out in the brief.

Nothing downstream starts on a guess: the core-100 proposal waits for the research, the article waits for the proposal to settle, and extraction waits for the proposal to be decided — which is what this page is for.

Decision S1

What principle decides whether something belongs in core?

The count is a consequence of the principle, not a target to hit by trimming. Every language on the research list has one, stated or implied, and they differ sharply.

Decision S2

What happens to the 1,129 compiler functions?

They must stay reachable or Hey stops self-hosting. They also have no business in a user's function table, and they are the single biggest contributor to the overflow.

Decision S3

Is there a blessed middle tier?

Every language on the list grew one, because "not core" and "unvetted stranger's code" are not the same claim. Go has golang.org/x; Ruby has default and bundled gems; Python has a shrinking equivalent.

Decision S4

Does extraction wait for the article?

The article is commissioned through the editorial process, with graphics showing what leaves the core. Publishing before the move means describing a plan; after means describing a fact.

Decision S5

When does the silent truncation get fixed?

Independent of everything above. Today, exceeding 2,048 prints a line and drops the definition. Whatever the core ends up being, that behaviour should not survive.
